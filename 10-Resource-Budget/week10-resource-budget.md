## Estimation Approach
I mainly used parametric estimating. I multiplied the number of weeks each person would work by their estimated hours per week and hourly rate. I used my Week 8 schedule to see when each role was needed and the Week 10 lecture for example rates.

1. I assumed it would take one week to roll out the system across all six clinics. If it takes a second week and the Project Manager and Developer work the same weekly hours, labor costs would increase by $5,725.
2. I assumed the QA Analyst would need 20 hours per week for two weeks to test the system and help clinic staff try it out. If they need 10 more hours, labor costs would increase by $900.

## Contingency Reserve
### Risk-Based Cost Exposure

- Risk 2: Intake and scheduling may not work together properly
- Potential Cost Exposure: $$3,100
- Risk 4: Appointment data may still contain errors after the planned cleanup
- Potential Cost Exposure: $1,740

**Recommended Contingency Reserve:** $4,840

I added the two estimates because both problems could come up before launch. The regular budget covers planned testing and cleanup. The reserve covers the extra work if either problem comes up.

### Largest Cost Category

Labor is the largest cost because the project needs people to build the intake and scheduling system, test it with clinic staff, and train employees at all six clinics.

---
## Budget Tradeoff Analysis

A 10% cut would save $4,209 and bring the budget down to $37,881. Before agreeing, I would check whether we could save that amount while still testing the system, training staff, and rolling it out to all six clinics.

1. I would check whether we could reuse RCHN’s existing testing tools or hosting. This could save money, but I would first make sure the existing setup can handle all the tests we need.
2. I would consider using the same training materials at all six clinics and combining some training sessions. This could reduce training hours, but it might make it harder for staff to attend without leaving their clinics short-staffed.

I would estimate the savings from each option before committing to the lower budget.
